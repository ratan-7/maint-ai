# Pump Maintenance Manual

## Excessive Vibration

### Possible Causes

- Bearing wear or damage
- Shaft misalignment
- Loose mounting bolts
- Coupling misalignment
- Pump imbalance
- Cavitation

### Inspection Steps

1. Check pump mounting bolts for looseness.
2. Inspect shaft and coupling alignment.
3. Inspect bearings for wear or unusual noise.
4. Check for excessive mechanical play.
5. Check suction conditions for possible cavitation.
6. Inspect the coupling for damage or misalignment.

### Evidence

Excessive vibration can be associated with mechanical looseness, bearing problems, misalignment, imbalance, or cavitation. Physical inspection is required to confirm the cause.

## High Temperature

### Possible Causes

- Bearing friction
- Insufficient lubrication
- Cooling problem
- Excessive load
- Misalignment

### Inspection Steps

1. Check bearing temperature.
2. Verify lubrication condition.
3. Inspect the cooling system.
4. Check pump operating load.
5. Inspect shaft and coupling alignment.

### Evidence

High operating temperature may indicate excessive friction, inadequate lubrication, cooling problems, or abnormal loading.

---

## Low Flow

### Possible Causes

- Blocked suction line
- Blocked filter
- Valve partially closed
- Pump operating outside its intended range
- Cavitation
- Impeller problem

### Inspection Steps

1. Check suction and discharge valves.
2. Inspect filters and strainers.
3. Check suction line for blockage.
4. Verify pump operating conditions.
5. Inspect the impeller if required.

### Evidence

Reduced flow can result from restrictions, incorrect valve position, operating conditions, or pump component problems.
